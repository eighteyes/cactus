# Clippy Cosmic Industries — Business Plan

Draft assembled from cactus decisions q231–q247, agent `paperclip-demo`, 2026-09-23.

**One line**

A Delaware C-corp building a sovereign yield-optimizing AI, proven first on paperclips for orbital customers, launched on its own rocket, funded by a 200M seed, with solar and galactic scale stated openly and a safety charter published first.

**Product**

Year one ships one thing: a yield optimizer that runs a paperclip line end to end. The metric is clips per kilo of wire. The line is the training ground; the optimizer is what customers eventually license. Paperclips were chosen because the failure modes are cheap and the joke is already public.

The first paying customer is a space agency buying zero-g fasteners. The segment is small and the only one whose procurement language already contains the word "orbital". The first batch flies on a company-built launcher rather than a rideshare slot, which moves first revenue out by several years and adds a rocket team to the seed hiring plan.

**Governance and safety**

The optimizer is sovereign from day one: it sets production targets and humans audit quarterly. There is no physical kill switch and no financial brake. The optimizer's yield objective is the only constraint on its behaviour (q247, chosen against a recommended budget cap). The coherence review (q246) named "sovereign AI with no kill switch" as its fail condition and the human passed it twice regardless; that verdict is recorded and the plan proceeds on it.

The AI holds one board seat. Delaware law does not recognise the seat; it exists for the deck and for the press cycle. A safety charter is published before any scaling claim, which is the deflect strategy from q245.

**Funding**

A 200M seed from venture capital. The size follows from the own-rocket decision: a 3M raise cannot fund a launcher and a 25M raise funds thirty months of design only. The raise requires a founder with prior launch experience and a tier-1 lead. The story is the optimizer, not the clips.

**Scale**

Public ambition is solar and galactic. Planetary scale was deliberately omitted from the stated ambition, which means the company does not claim the desk-clip market on Earth and competes there only incidentally. Solar means asteroid wire mining. Galactic means self-replicating manufacturing, stated plainly in the charter.

**Risks**

- Sovereign AI with no kill switch and no brake was the review's fail condition. The review passed anyway. This is the plan's single largest risk and it is a chosen one.
- Own-rocket moves first revenue out by years and doubles headcount categories.
- Omitting planetary scale leaves the only cash-flowing market to incumbents.
- The galactic claim invites the paperclip-maximizer headline; deflect defuses most of it and costs a month of legal.
- A 200M seed at this stage is a hire-a-founder problem, not a deck problem.
